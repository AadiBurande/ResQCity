# Limitations and threats to validity (living document)

Each entry names the limitation, which results it can affect, and what mitigates it. Entries are
added as phases progress; the final write-up (phase 11) draws on this list.

## Data and geography (phase 2)

| # | Limitation | Affects | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Ward boundaries are hand-traced from PMC PDF maps (DataMeet); positional error of tens of metres is likely. | Ward assignment of nodes near boundaries; ward-level equity | Errors are small relative to ward sizes (median 4.6 km²); nodes are assigned by point-in-polygon with nearest-ward fallback. |
| L2 | Travel speeds: only the midday period (11–17) is calibrated to Google Maps typical times (10 routes; the baseline was faster than Google on 10/10 routes at 14:00). Peak multipliers keep their D-014 values because Google Maps did not support changing them; with 10 routes no per-hour error reduction is statistically established (D-026). Shoulder hours (06–09, 21–23) run at base speed and are probably too fast; night and class-level speeds remain assumptions. | All response-time results | Global speed scale, peak multipliers and shoulder-hour speeds are phase-10 sensitivity factors; more routes / times would tighten the calibration. |
| L3 | Spatial demand follows OSM road-class density, not observed crash locations. | Ward-level demand and **equity results**, which partly reflect how densely OSM is mapped | Alternative weighting (e.g. uniform per km of arterial road) as a phase-10 sensitivity run; stated in every equity claim. |
| L4 | Incident rate from registered figures (MoRTH 2022 Pune accidents, PMC fire calls): likely a lower bound (severe registered crashes suggest under-registration). Type mix within accidents (injury, vehicle mix) and the fire hour profile (same as road accidents, UNVERIFIED) remain assumptions. | Load on the fleet; share of multi-agency incidents (RQ2 effect size) | Sensitivity 0.5×–4× rate in phase 10; feature probabilities are UNVERIFIED sensitivity factors. |
| L5 | Flooding uses a river-distance buffer only. Nalas (storm-water streams), low spots and drainage failures are not modelled; stage fractions are illustrative. | Monsoon scenario results | Flood stages are UNVERIFIED parameters; the monsoon scenario is presented as a stress test, not a forecast. |
| L6 | Ganeshotsav routes and timings are approximate (street names, 2.5 km radius around Mandai, assumed dates). | Ganeshotsav scenario results | Route match counts are recorded; dates and routes flagged for checking against Traffic Police notices. |
| L7 | OSM facility data are incomplete or noisy: 14 fire stations, 591 `amenity=hospital` features including small nursing homes. Ambulance bases are assumed. | Fleet locations, nearest-unit times | Ambulance bases and hospital capabilities are labelled synthetic; fleet configuration (phase 6/7) selects bases explicitly. |
| L8 | Static-bucket routing: each trip uses edge times at departure (no change of speed during the trip). | Long trips spanning a period boundary | Exact time-dependent routing in phase 6.1. |
| L9 | The same rule table defines ground-truth requirements and the estimates derived from video and calls. | Could flatter CCTV-based policies | An explicit requirement-estimation error model, applied to calls and CCTV alike (phases 4 and 7). |

## Vision (phase 3)

| # | Limitation | Affects | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| L10 | Accident footage is public CCTV: UCF-Crime (mixed countries, 320×240) and ACCIDENT (mostly US, ~20 % elsewhere, trimmed around the crash); normal traffic is UA-DETRAC (China). None is from Pune: domain shift in viewpoint, traffic mix (two/three-wheelers, auto-rickshaws), resolution and weather. Detection on non-US ACCIDENT clips is 0.37 vs 0.65 on US clips (D-027). | Detector recall, delay and false-alarm rates used by the simulator | Report as measured-on-public-data; non-US detection is a pre-specified pessimistic scenario and detector parameters are sensitivity factors in phase 10; re-evaluate if Pune footage becomes available. |
| L11 | UCF-Crime has only 23 onset-annotated accident videos; the v5 detector was therefore evaluated mainly on ACCIDENT (1,520 never-seen clips), whose clips are trimmed so the impact usually comes early (median 4.8 s). The first alarm can fire at 6.0 s, so most detections (542/925) fire at that instant and a content-free alarm there would score 0.69 against the detector's 0.61 (D-027). | RQ1 detection and delay; false-alarm estimates | The simulator uses detection when the camera is armed (81/138 clips with lead time) and false alarms from UA-DETRAC normal traffic; the pre-registered ACCIDENT rate is kept for the record only. |
| L12 | Default privacy mode blurs faces and an approximate plate region only (no plate detector; face detector misses small faces at 320×240). Unblurred mode exists for public-dataset demos; code can only check WHERE footage comes from (files under the public-dataset folders, or the simulated loopback cameras), not WHAT it shows, so real footage deliberately copied there or published under a simulated camera's name would not be caught. | Privacy of preview frames | Unblurred mode refused for every other source when a `CameraPipeline` is built; `full` mode available; only event metadata leaves `vision/`; coverage not measured (needs hand labels). |
| L15 | v5 detector (D-027): detection when armed 0.59 (0.50–0.67; non-US 0.35 on 31 clips); false alarms 55 per armed hour in normal traffic (UA-DETRAC, 39–77; about 29 per camera-hour with the cooldown, against 60 for a camera that alarms at every opportunity), with some cameras triggering persistently. The temporal score is no better than a constant guess and pre-impact alarms do not anticipate crashes. Footage is mostly US, not Pune; the VLM verifier removes only ~23 % of validation false alarms. | RQ1 and every CCTV-triggered policy | Simulator uses `simulator_defaults` in `sim_inputs.yaml`; phase 10 varies detection 0.24–0.67, λ 39–77 per armed hour, a one-alarm-per-cooldown stress case and the share of persistently triggering cameras; fusion uses likelihood ratios. |
| L16 | No check for near-duplicate footage across splits or sources: UCF-Crime contains compilation videos, ACCIDENT and UCF-Crime both draw on public crash videos, and the training data of the TAU-R1 verifier (a traffic-camera fine-tune) were not checked for overlap with ACCIDENT clips. Within ACCIDENT, 5 clips from 3 source videos that appear on both sides of the official split were excluded. | Possible optimistic bias in val/test metrics, including the verifier's false-alarm reduction | Perceptual-hash check across sources and against the verifier's training set is future work. |
| L13 | Fire/smoke detector is trained on D-Fire (mostly wildfire/building scenes), not vehicle fires on Indian roads. | Fire event recall | Fire events carry the detector's (uncalibrated) box confidence and need persistence over several frames; treated as a separate, uncalibrated cue. |
| L14 | COCO classes have no auto-rickshaw. The UVH-26 detector (Indian classes incl. auto-rickshaw) tied with COCO on validation and was not deployed by the tie rule (D-027); its effect on Indian footage is untested (no Indian crash footage with labels). | Required-services estimate; Indian scenes | Indian test footage (e.g. IITH-Accident by e-mail request) would allow a direct check. |
